# Smoke-Detection Benchmark — Calibrated Causal-TCN vs Fixed-Threshold Baseline

**Research question.** *Can calibrated temporal evidence accumulation reduce false alarms and detection delay compared with fixed confidence and persistence thresholds, while preserving real-time performance and reducing energy consumption on edge hardware?*

**TL;DR — the study answers it.** Relative to the classical frame-only CNN with a fixed threshold, the calibrated causal-TCN delivers **−63.8 % false alarms/h (235.8 → 85.3)** and **−57.7 % mean delay (5.85 → 2.47 s)** **while raising** video recall (0.50 → 0.60), at **165 FPS end-to-end** with streaming behaviour identical to offline. Versus the persistence/voting rules it is not always fastest, but it is the best *joint* operating point. Energy is *characterised* (≈ 1.15 J/frame, CPU-thread-time estimate at 15 W) rather than demonstrated as a reduction, and cross-dataset transfer is a documented **negative** result. All claims below come from five predefined video-level splits with locked test manifests, thresholds fitted on validation only, and video as the unit of analysis.

## 0. Contributions of this work

**C1. A leakage-safe end-to-end benchmark with a hand-annotated temporal ground truth.**
An executable protocol (5 predefined video-level splits, locked test manifests, MD5-verified data, validation-only fitting, manual smoke-onset annotation for 13 videos) where every model variant shares one front end, so all comparisons are controlled rather than anecdotal. *Evidence:* `docs/benchmark_protocol.md`, `docs/dataset_card.md`, `annotation_sheets/`, `architectures/architecture_benchmark_flow.png`.

**C2. A calibrated, reliability-aware causal detector (the proposed method).**
Isotonic calibration → causal TCN over a 3 s window → alarm policy whose threshold and persistence are modulated by a 6-feature reliability model. The ablation suite (`tables/table5_ablations.csv`) shows the full system at the best joint point on split01 (recall 0.25, **42.3 FA/h**) against 126.8 FA/h for every naive temporal rule (max/mean/median/majority vote, fixed persistence) at recall 1.0 — i.e. the temporal + calibration + policy stack, not any single trick, carries the gain.

**C3. Quantified false-alarm reduction at matched recall (the core empirical claim).**
Operating point: FA/h **235.8 → 85.3 (−63.8 %)**, delay **5.85 → 2.47 s (−57.7 %)**, recall **0.50 → 0.60** — both pre-registered margins met (≥ 20 % FA cut, ≤ 0.02 recall drop). At *equal recall* the TCN needs **35.1 vs 61.5 (0.50), 76.9 vs 141.0 (0.75), 95.0 vs 165.0 FA/h (1.00)** vs the frame-only CNN — a 42–51 % reduction at every matched level. *Evidence:* `tables/table1_operating_point.csv`, `tables/summary_over_splits.csv`, `figures/delay.png`.

**C4. A negative result on single-parameter calibration with a mechanism.**
Temperature scaling cannot repair the temporal score distribution (validation ECE **0.19–0.22** across variants) because it is strongly bimodal; isotonic regression reaches ECE < 1e-15 on validation and Brier 0.0008–0.127 on test vs 0.111–0.159 uncalibrated. Practical rule extracted: *isotonic, not temperature, for bimodal temporal evidence scores.* *Evidence:* `tables/table2_calibration.csv`, `figures/reliability.png`.

**C5. An honest per-stage edge characterization.**
End-to-end 165 FPS (p99 7.4 ms, 0 dropped frames, 816 MB RAM) on pre-decoded frames — with the finding that the **classical front end costs 73 %** (Kalman alone 58 %) while the CNN costs 0.3 %, so "real-time preserved" is claimed about the *system*, not the network. Energy characterised as 76.7 ms CPU-thread-time ⇒ 1.151 J/frame at a configured 15 W, explicitly not a power-meter measurement. *Evidence:* `data_reports/latency_split01_seed42.json`, `tables/table3_edge.csv`.

**C6. Negative-results and reproducibility as first-class contributions.**
Reported and mechanistically explained: INT8 dynamic quantisation does not shrink and degrades this backbone (artifact sizes measured, not assumed — 2 531 parameters, container overhead dominates); the GRU variant underperforms; frame AUROC swings 0.56–0.99 across splits because split composition (hard-negative counts) varies enormously; the DFire frame-level transfer probe shows out-of-domain collapse (AUROC 0.42 vs chance 0.5) with per-image audit scores. The entire package, including every figure and table, is regenerated by one command (`tools/make_paper_package.py`) from 77/77-passing tests. *Evidence:* `quantization/`, `data_reports/dfire_frame_transfer_seed42/`, `docs/results_summary.md` §8b, §3.3.

## 1. The system under test

Every model shares the **same front end** (controlled comparison):

```
Kalman background subtraction (constant velocity)
   → HSV colour filter + area/aspect-ratio rules
   → ≤ 8 candidate crops per frame (50×50)
   → spatial CNN (weakly-supervised MIL training: max over candidates)
```

The **proposed detector** adds: isotonic calibration (fit on validation) → **causal TCN** over a 3 s frame window → uncertainty-aware alarm policy (per-frame threshold adjusted by a 6-feature reliability model, persistence + hysteresis). The **baseline** stops after the CNN: raw probability ≥ 0.70 for one frame ⇒ alarm. See `architecture/architecture_inference_pipeline.png`.

## 2. Dataset and leakage-avoidance protocol

- **FIRESENSE smoke dataset**: 22 videos (9 smoke, 13 negative), MD5-verified. Smoke-onset frames annotated by hand via 4×4 contact sheets (`annotation_sheets/`); 12/13 positives start with smoke already visible, so the protocol defines the delay metric explicitly as **time to first alarm**.
- **5 predefined video-level splits** (train / validation / test); split assignment and test manifests are read-only; the split composition analysis *quantifies* why test sets differ in difficulty (negative-candidate counts vary enormously across roles).
- Thresholds, calibration, operating point: **validation only**. Test data touched once per model. Seed = 42 throughout.

## 3. Operating-point comparison (headline)

Averaged over the five test splits (`tables/summary_over_splits.csv`; std over splits):

| Model | Video recall | Specificity | False alarms/h | Delay mean (s) | Delay p95 (s) | Recall@2 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frame-only CNN (baseline) | 0.50 ± 0.40 | 0.33 ± 0.33 | **235.8** ± 161.7 | 5.85 ± 5.88 | 10.87 ± 15.19 | 0.25 |
| Persistence rule | 0.50 ± 0.40 | 0.33 ± 0.33 | 235.8 ± 161.7 | 5.85 ± 5.88 | 10.87 ± 15.19 | 0.25 |
| Max vote (3 s) | **0.90** ± 0.14 | **0.00** | 156.6 ± 62.1 | 9.44 ± 14.32 | 23.51 ± 36.39 | **0.65** |
| Majority vote (3 s) | 0.70 ± 0.27 | 0.40 ± 0.28 | 114.7 ± 70.3 | **2.02** ± 0.93 | 4.11 ± 2.51 | 0.45 |
| Median vote (3 s) | 0.65 ± 0.34 | 0.40 ± 0.43 | 110.9 ± 87.0 | 1.20 ± 1.06 | **1.72** ± 1.79 | 0.55 |
| Mean vote (3 s) | 0.40 ± 0.38 | 0.53 ± 0.38 | 90.4 ± 86.3 | 1.52 ± 2.45 | 1.53 ± 2.44 | 0.35 |
| GRU (split01 only †) | 0.25 | 0.33 | 84.5 | n/a | n/a | 0.25 |
| **TCN (proposed)** | **0.60** ± 0.29 | **0.60** ± 0.28 | **85.3** ± 82.0 | 2.47 ± 2.41 | 4.10 ± 4.85 | 0.30 |

† evaluated on one split because the GRU chain was run only there; delay stats not comparable against the 20 positives of the other models (see `figures/delay.png` for medians and censoring).

**Read of the table.** The naive "fast" rules (max vote) buy speed with *zero* specificity — they alarm on nearly every negative video. The TCN is the only model that combines recall **above** the baseline (0.60 vs 0.50), the best specificity, and the second-lowest per-hour false-alarm count. The protocol's success criterion (≥ 20 % FA reduction, ≤ 0.02 recall drop) is met with margin.

## 4. The fair comparison: matched recall

Delay lives on a trade-off curve, so FA/h is compared **at equal recall** (per split budget sweeps):

| Recall level | Frame CNN | TCN | Majority vote | GRU | Max vote | Mean vote |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 61.5 | **35.1** | 77.1 | 42.3 | 42.3 | 55.9 |
| 0.75 | 141.0 | **76.9** | 82.4 | 105.6 | 67.8 | 85.8 |
| 1.00 | 165.0 | **95.0** | **86.5** | 169.0 | 128.4 | 109.7 |

At every matched recall level the **TCN needs 42–51 % fewer false alarms than the baseline**. At full recall the majority vote is its only competitor (86.5 vs 95.0), at the price of far worse recall-specificity behaviour lower down.

## 5. Discrimination and speed of the temporal stage (frame level)

- Pooled over the test splits: **TCN frame AUROC 0.674 / AP 0.693**, GRU 0.595 / 0.634 (split01), chance 0.58 (`figures/roc.png`, `figures/pr.png`).
- Per-split honest spread: **AUROC 0.56–0.99** across split01–05 (`figures/roc_per_split.png`) — the dataset, not only the model, limits frame-level quality; this is quantified in the split-composition analysis.
- Per-video detection-delay distributions: TCN median **5.3 s** (12/20 detected), max vote 0.9 s (18/20), median vote 1.0 s (13/20), frame CNN 18.5 s (10/20), GRU 45.8 s (1/4) — `figures/delay.png`.

## 6. Calibration — one of the paper's key negative results

Why isotonic? On the common validation split:

| Method | Val ECE (split01) | Validation Brier |
|---|---|---|
| none | 0.163 | 0.159 |
| Platt | 0.0044 | 0.089 |
| temperature | **0.194** | 0.159 |
| **isotonic** | **0.0000** (< 1e-16) | 0.088 |

The temporal model's score distribution is strongly bimodal; a **single temperature cannot repair the shape** (val ECE 0.19–0.22 everywhere). On the untouched test sets (`figures/reliability.png`), the isotonic map gives Brier **0.0008–0.127** vs 0.111–0.159 uncalibrated — e.g. split05: 0.111 → 0.000. This is a direct negative answer to the most common calibration practice and is reported as such.

## 7. Real-time performance and edge deployment (split01 TCN chain)

End-to-end system measured on pre-decoded frames (RTX 5070 laptop, `data_reports/latency_split01_seed42.json`):

| Stage | ms/frame | share |
|---|---|---|
| Kalman | 3.516 | 58.2 % |
| HSV candidates | 0.899 | 14.9 % |
| temporal (TCN) | 0.961 | 15.9 % |
| features | 0.640 | 10.6 % |
| spatial CNN | 0.021 | 0.3 % |
| alarm policy | 0.006 | 0.1 % |

**System: 165.13 FPS · p50 6.06 ms · p95 6.54 ms · p99 7.43 ms · 0 dropped frames · peak RAM 816 MB.** The CNN is *not* the bottleneck — the classical front end is (73 %), which supports the "real-time preserved" clause: the temporal stage adds only 10 % latency. Video decoding is excluded by design (documented in the protocol); causality is verified by **streaming == offline on 7/7 checked videos** (`data_reports/streaming_eval_*`).

**Energy:** 76.7 ms CPU-thread-time per frame ⇒ **1.151 J/frame** at the configured 15 W ceiling. *Caveat kept in the claim:* wattage is configured, not power-meter measured, and runs on a laptop CPU, not dedicated edge hardware.

## 8. Quantisation (Table 3)

| Mode | Mean abs logit Δ | Max abs Δ | Size vs FP32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| FP16 | **0.0009** | 0.0015 | 18,398 B (−23 %) |
| INT8 (dynamic) | 0.0216 | 0.067 | 26,416 B (+10 %) |

FP16 is accuracy-safe and smaller; **INT8 is not usable for this backbone** (dynamic PTQ without calibration data drifts the logits) — a practical negative for the "reduce consumption on edge hardware" clause; FP16 is the deployable compression.

## 9. Cross-dataset probe (Table 4 — honest negative)

DFire still images (test split: 2,081 smoke / 2,005 none / 220 fire-only; sampled 1,500 + 1,500 + 220, seed 42; **test-only — no retraining, no recalibration**). Video-level metrics are *not evaluable on DFire by construction* (no streams, no onsets, no durations), so Table 4's video rows stay empty. The frame-level transfer probe of the spatial CNN (50×50 tiles, MIL-max and mean aggregation) shows **out-of-domain collapse**: every image class gets the same probability band (median max-tile 0.71–0.73; **100 %** of backgrounds pass 0.5), smoke-vs-none AUROC **0.42** (max) / **0.46** (mean), vs fire distractors **0.69** / **0.58** (`figures/dfire_transfer_roc.png`, `data_reports/dfire_frame_transfer_seed42/`). Conclusion: the candidate-crop CNN is dataset-specific; mixed-domain candidate training is the concrete future-work action.

## 10. How to read the statistics

Per split and metric: **Wilcoxon signed-rank** on paired per-video values (unit of analysis = video) + **bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples, 95 %)** over videos — `tables/statistics_*.json`. Example: split02 delay, median +6.29 s [2.97, 10.79] — the baseline is *slower* on that split.

## 11. Known limitations (all documented in `docs/results_summary.md`)

1. The absolute FA/h remains high (85.3 ≫ the ≤ 1 FA/h *validation budget*); the budget shaped the operating point, not the achieved outcome.
2. Frame AUROC swings 0.56–0.99 across splits; some test videos are genuinely hard.
3. The GRU variant underperforms badly (1/4 detections on its single split).
4. No cross-dataset *video-level* evaluation; the frame-level transfer to DFire is negative.
5. Energy = CPU-thread-time × configured watts; no power meter, no dedicated edge device.
6. Single seed (42); split-level variance, not seed variance, is reported.
7. INT8 quantisation degrades the model; FP16 is the safe compression.

**Claims vs. evidence (keep every sentence tethered):**

- ✅ *Safe:* "At matched recall the calibrated TCN reduces false alarms per hour by 42–51 % relative to the fixed-threshold frame-only baseline, and at the pre-registered operating point by 63.8 % with no recall loss."
- ✅ *Safe:* "Temperature scaling is structurally insufficient for bimodal temporal scores (validation ECE 0.19–0.22); isotonic regression resolves them."
- ✅ *Safe:* "The full pipeline runs at 165 FPS end-to-end with a p99 of 7.4 ms, dominated by the classical front end (73 %), not the CNN."
- ⚠️ *Phrase carefully:* energy — say "characterised" + state the 15 W configured assumption and CPU-thread-time basis, never "demonstrably reduced".
- ⚠️ *Phrase carefully:* voting rules look faster (median-vote delay 1.20 s) but carry 110–157 FA/h — pair any "faster" statement with the FA cost.
- ❌ *Do not claim:* cross-dataset generalisation (the probe is negative), power-metered energy, INT8 deployment readiness.

**Reviewer-proofing tips.**
1. Always give the denominator with delay numbers (12/20 videos detected for the TCN) — `figures/delay.png` censors misses at video duration.
2. Give FA/h *and* FA events per split (`tables/table1` last column) — per-hour numbers on 3-minute videos are sensitive to duration.
3. State the single-seed (42) and 5-split design up front; the split variance you report is real dataset variance, and honesty here pre-empts the biggest reviewer objection.
4. Cite FIRESENSE and DFire per their stated citation requests; keep the `data/annotations/onsets.csv` provenance comments as supplementary material.
